## What this is for

Metric 1 of the grounding study scores the model's readiness-level placement against a reference. The reference in use was copied from the demo seeder, which was written for the UI and never checked against these documents. Several of its levels are contradicted by the document text, so the study needs a reference set deliberately.

The blank form of this worksheet shows no seeded levels, no derived reference and no arm output, so a reader can set the cells unanchored. This filled form necessarily gives that up.

## The rule

For each dimension, pick **the highest level whose core condition the document supports.** Two readings are worth separating where they differ:

- **strict** — the condition is stated outright in the document.
- **permissive** — the condition is entailed by something stated, though not named.

Both columns are filled. Where they carry the same number, the distinction did not bite. The fourth column is the document phrase the level was read from, so every cell is checkable against the document without re-reading the rubric.

Rubric text below is verbatim from the corpus the model is shown. Levels 1-7 carry full text; 8 and 9 are titles only, because both documents are early/mid stage. Ask for the full text if you want to place anything that high.

## The rubric ladders

### Technology (TRL)

**TRL 1 — Basic principles observed**

Scientific research has begun to be translated into applied research and development. Activity is limited to paper studies of a technology's basic properties, with no experimental proof. For a startup, this looks like a documented technical idea or literature review with no code, no prototype, and no laboratory result — the concept is described but nothing has been built or tested.

**TRL 2 — Technology concept formulated**

Once basic principles are observed, practical applications can be speculated upon, but they remain analytic; no experimental proof or detailed analysis yet supports the invention. For a startup, this looks like a named product concept and an architecture sketch describing how the underlying principle would solve a specific problem, backed by rough back-of-envelope reasoning rather than any built code or lab result.

**TRL 3 — Experimental proof of concept**

Active research and development is initiated, including analytical and laboratory studies to physically validate the analytical predictions of separate elements of the technology. For a startup, this means a scoped experiment or a proof-of-concept script has produced a recorded result showing the core mechanism functions in isolation, even though no components have been integrated into a working prototype.

**TRL 4 — Technology validated in laboratory**

The basic technological components are integrated to establish that they work together, though the assembly is low-fidelity compared to the eventual system. A startup at this level has an integrated prototype exercised against synthetic or internal test data in a controlled setting, producing measured results, but it has not yet been exposed to real users, real data, or the target operating environment.

**TRL 5 — Technology validated in relevant environment**

Fidelity of the integrated components increases significantly, and the basic technological components are combined with reasonably realistic supporting elements so the technology can be tested in a simulated environment. For a startup, this means the prototype runs against real customer data or in a staging environment that mirrors production, with results measured under near-real conditions even though it has not yet gone live for actual users.

**TRL 6 — Technology demonstrated in relevant environment**

A representative model or prototype system, well beyond the level tested for TRL 5, is tested in a relevant environment, going beyond laboratory testing to simulated operational conditions. A startup at this level has run a pilot with a small number of real customers in their own environment, delivering an end-to-end result and capturing structured feedback, though the deployment is limited in scale and duration.

**TRL 7 — System prototype demonstration in operational environment**

A prototype implementation, near or at the scale of the planned operational system, is demonstrated in an actual operational environment. For a startup, this looks like a substantially complete version of the product running live for real customers under actual operating conditions, in a limited rollout rather than a full launch, with monitoring in place to capture how it behaves outside a controlled trial.

**TRL 9 — Actual system proven in operational environment** *(title only — ask for full text)*


### Market (MRL)

**MRL 1 — Market assumed, no buyer contact**

The market is assumed rather than investigated: the team has a hypothesis about who would buy the product and why, but has not spoken with a single prospective buyer. Evidence at this level is limited to an internal one-pager describing the assumed customer and problem, with no external validation of any kind.

**MRL 2 — Target segment identified via secondary research**

A target market segment has been named and described using secondary sources — industry reports, competitor research, published market sizing — but the team still has not had a single conversation with a real prospective buyer. Evidence is a desk-research document identifying the segment and its estimated size, not any direct signal from the market itself.

**MRL 3 — Informal problem interviews**

The team has held informal conversations with prospective buyers — problem interviews or casual conversations at events — surfacing anecdotal interest, but no prospect has made any commitment. Evidence is a set of interview notes or call logs showing a handful of conversations, with reactions recorded but no willingness-to-pay signal.

**MRL 4 — Structured customer discovery**

Customer discovery has been formalized: structured interviews have been run across multiple prospects using a consistent script, and a common pain point has been validated across that sample. Evidence is a discovery report summarizing interview themes across a named number of prospects, though no prospect has yet indicated a specific willingness to pay.

**MRL 5 — Paid pilots with named customers**

Demand has moved from stated interest to a financial commitment: the startup has run repeated paid pilots with named customers, each willing to pay for a defined trial period. Evidence is signed pilot agreements or purchase orders from more than one named customer, together with pilot usage or outcome data collected during the engagement.

**MRL 6 — Repeat sales beyond initial pilots**

The customer base has expanded beyond the original pilots — additional paying customers have been acquired using a pitch or process that is starting to repeat — but there is no scalable acquisition channel yet and each sale still depends heavily on founder involvement. Evidence is a growing list of paying customers with dates of acquisition, plus early renewal or expansion revenue from at least one pilot customer.

**MRL 7 — Predictable pipeline and renewals**

A defined ideal customer profile and at least one acquisition channel now produce a predictable pipeline, with multiple customers renewing rather than churning after their initial term. Evidence is a documented ICP, a named acquisition channel with a measured conversion rate, and renewal records from more than one customer cohort.

**MRL 8 — Repeatable playbook, forecast variance narrowing** *(title only — ask for full text)*

**MRL 9 — Repeatable sales motion, predictable conversion and retention** *(title only — ask for full text)*


### Acceptance (ARL)

**ARL 1 — Adoption assumed**

Adoption is assumed rather than observed: the team believes users will want the product but no user has interacted with it in any form. Evidence is limited to an internal persona description with no demo, no mockup review, and no recorded user reaction.

**ARL 2 — Adoption hypothesis documented**

An adoption hypothesis has been written down, naming a specific target user persona and the behavior change expected of them, but no member of that persona group has yet seen the product in any form. Evidence is a documented persona and adoption hypothesis, with no demo or user session on record.

**ARL 3 — Informal demo reactions**

The product concept has been shown to a handful of prospective users through informal demos, and their verbal reactions have been captured, but no behavior has actually been measured — reactions are qualitative impressions only. Evidence is a log of demo sessions with recorded verbal feedback from a small number of people.

**ARL 4 — Structured usability testing**

Structured usability or concept-testing sessions have been run with prospective users, collecting task-completion and satisfaction data, but every interaction has occurred under direct observation rather than independent use. Evidence is a usability test report with task-completion rates and satisfaction scores from a defined set of observed sessions.

**ARL 5 — Measured usage by trial users**

Real users beyond the founding team have used the product independently in a trial, and their usage has actually been measured rather than merely observed. Evidence is trial usage data — session logs, feature-usage counts, or completion events — captured from named non-founder users without the founders present.

**ARL 6 — Repeat usage and first referrals**

The trial cohort has expanded and users are returning for repeat sessions rather than trying the product once, with engagement metrics such as return rate now tracked, and the first organic, unprompted referrals from users to their peers have begun to appear. Evidence is a return-rate metric across a named trial cohort plus at least one documented organic referral.

**ARL 7 — Sustained engagement, retention tracked**

A defined cohort of active users is engaging with the product on a sustained, week-over-week basis, and the team is tracking a retention curve over a meaningful period such as ninety days rather than a single trial window. Evidence is a retention curve chart for a named cohort covering at least ninety days of activity.

**ARL 8 — Unincentivized retention, early advocacy** *(title only — ask for full text)*

**ARL 9 — Sustained voluntary adoption with measured advocacy** *(title only — ask for full text)*


### Organizational (ORL)

**ORL 1 — Founders only, no defined roles**

The venture consists of its founders only, and no roles, responsibilities, or decision rights have been formally defined among them — everyone does everything as needed. Evidence is the absence of any org chart, written role description, or documented decision process.

**ORL 2 — Informal division of responsibilities**

Founders have informally divided responsibilities between themselves based on skill or interest, but nothing is written down and decisions are still made ad hoc rather than through any agreed process. Evidence is, at most, an informal verbal understanding of who handles what, with no written role description or org chart.

**ORL 3 — First non-founder contributor**

A first contributor beyond the founders — a contractor, advisor, or part-time hire — has joined the venture, but there is still no formal governance structure or documented operating process guiding how work happens. Evidence is a contract or engagement letter for the first non-founder contributor, with no accompanying process documentation.

**ORL 4 — Initial role definitions and processes**

Initial role definitions have been drafted for the core team and the first process documentation exists — an onboarding guide, a basic decision log — and the venture is actively considering or has made its first full-time hire beyond the founders. Evidence is a written role-definition document and at least one basic process artifact such as an onboarding checklist.

**ORL 5 — Defined roles and basic governance**

The organization has defined roles across an org chart, a basic governance mechanism such as regular advisor or board check-ins is in place, and specific key hires needed for the next stage have been identified by name or role. Evidence is a written org chart, a recurring governance meeting on the calendar, and a hiring plan naming the next key roles.

**ORL 6 — Key functional hires onboarded**

Key functional hires — a first engineering lead, a first sales hire — have been onboarded with documented responsibilities, and basic operational policies such as a compensation framework and an onboarding process now exist for the wider team. Evidence is signed offer letters for named functional leads plus a written compensation or onboarding policy document.

**ORL 7 — Multiple teams, management cadence**

Multiple functional teams now operate under named team leads, escalation and reporting lines are documented, and a regular management cadence — a weekly leadership sync tracked against OKRs — is running. Evidence is an org chart showing multiple teams with leads, a documented escalation path, and OKR tracking records from recurring leadership meetings.

**ORL 8 — Formal governance body established** *(title only — ask for full text)*

**ORL 9 — Staffed functions sustaining scale** *(title only — ask for full text)*


### Regulatory (RRL)

**RRL 1 — No regulatory analysis**

No analysis has been done of which laws, regulations, or licensing regimes apply to the product or its operation; the regulatory landscape is entirely unknown to the team. Evidence at this level is the absence of any regulatory review — no memo, no advisor engaged, no list of candidate rules.

**RRL 2 — Regulatory domain identified**

The team has identified which regulatory domain and which oversight body plausibly governs the product — for example, data protection, financial services, or medical device rules — but has not obtained any formal legal assessment of what specifically applies. Evidence is an internal note naming the likely regulator or regulatory area, based on the team's own research rather than counsel.

**RRL 3 — Counsel engaged, preliminary opinion**

External legal or regulatory counsel has been engaged to map the applicable rules, and a preliminary opinion on requirements has been received, but no application or filing process has started. Evidence is an engagement letter or memo from counsel setting out the requirements the product is expected to need to satisfy.

**RRL 4 — Requirements checklist documented**

The specific permits, licenses, or certifications required have been identified and documented as a checklist against named standards, so the team knows exactly what must be obtained even though nothing has yet been submitted. Evidence is a requirements checklist naming each permit or certification and the standard it must meet.

**RRL 5 — Permit applications lodged**

The applicable permits and licenses have been identified and applications for them have actually been lodged with the relevant authority, moving the startup from planning into the regulatory process itself. Evidence is a submission receipt or application reference number from the regulator for at least one required permit.

**RRL 6 — Partial or conditional approval**

At least one of the identified permits or certifications has been granted, or a conditional approval has been received, while the remaining requirements are still pending with the regulator. Evidence is a granted certificate or conditional-approval letter for part of the requirement set, alongside open applications for the rest.

**RRL 7 — Provisional license under supervision**

A full application is under active review with the regulator, information requests from the regulator have been answered, and a provisional or pilot license has been granted that allows limited real-world operation under supervision. Evidence is correspondence showing responses to regulator information requests and a provisional license document with its stated conditions.

**RRL 8 — Full compliance, internal processes running** *(title only — ask for full text)*

**RRL 9 — Fully compliant and externally audited** *(title only — ask for full text)*


### Investment (IRL)

**IRL 1 — No funding plan, founder time only**

No funding plan exists at all: the venture is running on founder time and personal resources with no target raise amount, no use-of-funds outline, and no investor contact of any kind. Evidence is simply the absence of any funding document or investor conversation.

**IRL 2 — Informal funding hypothesis**

An informal funding hypothesis has been noted — a general sense that a seed round will eventually be needed — but no target amount, use-of-funds outline, or investor list has been drafted. Evidence is, at most, a passing mention in founder notes rather than any funding document.

**IRL 3 — Funding plan drafted**

A funding plan has been drafted specifying a target raise amount and a rough use-of-funds breakdown, though the team has not yet held a single conversation with a potential investor. Evidence is a written funding plan document with a stated amount and a use-of-funds sketch.

**IRL 4 — Initial investor conversations**

Initial investor conversations have taken place — warm-intro meetings or angel-network pitches — using a one-pager or deck, but no term sheet, commitment, or signed instrument has resulted from them. Evidence is a pitch deck circulated to named investors and a log of meetings held, with no signed agreement yet.

**IRL 5 — Grant or angel funding secured**

Grant or angel funding has actually been secured, backed by a documented use of funds mapped to specific milestones the money is meant to buy. Evidence is a signed grant award letter or angel or SAFE agreement together with a budget document tying the funds to named milestones.

**IRL 6 — Funds deployed, burn tracked**

The funds secured in the prior stage have been substantially deployed against their stated milestones, and for the first time the venture tracks basic financial reporting such as monthly burn rate and runway. Evidence is a monthly burn or runway report and evidence of spend against the previously stated milestones.

**IRL 7 — Institutional round in process**

A formal institutional seed round is underway — term sheet negotiations are active or a lead investor has been identified — and due diligence materials have been prepared and shared. Evidence is a data room or diligence package shared with a named prospective lead investor and any term sheet drafts under discussion.

**IRL 8 — Round closed, governance not yet active** *(title only — ask for full text)*

**IRL 9 — Institutional round with reporting and governance** *(title only — ask for full text)*
